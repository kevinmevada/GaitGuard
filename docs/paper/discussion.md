# Discussion

## 1. Principal findings

The primary, unaffected endpoint of this study is the healthy-reference anomaly-screening ensemble, which achieves ROC-AUC 0.7545 under strict leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) across all eight Voisard cohorts without using any pathological labels during training. Isolation Forest on BiLSTM-AE latents is the effective in-domain detector (AUC 0.7540); the source-locked 2-method rank-averaged ensemble adds negligible lift (+0.0005) and is reported for protocol consistency.

The provisional supervised pathology-tier classifier shows a pronounced tier gradient — F1 of 0.857 for neurological cohorts versus 0.622 for orthopedic and 0.526 for Healthy (XGBoost; macro-F1 0.669, macro one-vs-rest AUC 0.842) — indicating the model discriminates neurological gait signatures substantially more reliably than orthopedic ones, and struggles comparatively with the Healthy class itself. This asymmetry is directionally plausible (neurological gait pathology often produces more global, systemic movement changes than localized orthopedic impairment) but, given this classifier's provisional status (Results §2b; Methods secondary protocol; Limitations), we present this pattern as a hypothesis for the corrected Voisard-only re-run to confirm rather than a settled finding.

## 2. Interpretation of model behavior

Global SHAP importance on the primary tabular classifier is dominated by lower-back signal complexity: `lb_sampen_mean` (mean absolute SHAP 0.381) ranks first by a wide margin over the next two features (`it_ms_mp_29_mean`, 0.281; `it_ms_mp_27_mean`, 0.164). This is corroborated by feature-ablation results: removing trunk-dynamics features drops nested-RFECV AUC from 0.8489 to 0.7980 (Δ = −0.051), the single largest degradation among all tested feature-family removals — every other `minus_*` ablation leaves AUC materially unchanged, and restricting to only the top-10 SHAP features costs just 0.023 AUC (0.8489 → 0.8255). Together, these results converge on the same signal: lower-back/trunk movement complexity carries most of the classifier's discriminative information, while many other engineered features are redundant with it rather than independently informative. *(Provisional — same tabular contamination as Results §2b.)*

## 3. Relation between classical and deep approaches

Among six deep architectures evaluated under matched LOSO protocol (clean N=260; DAPHNET LB-only trials excluded by multi-sensor loading), the Gait Transformer achieved the highest AUC (0.950, 95% CI [0.929, 0.970]), followed by TCN (0.941), InceptionTime (0.936), CNN-1D (0.934), BiLSTM-Attention (0.933), and DeepConvLSTM (0.926) — all six cluster within a narrow 0.024 AUC band, with overlapping confidence intervals across the top four. We do not identify TCN as the strongest deep model; that distinction belongs to the Transformer architecture in this evaluation. All six supervised deep models outperform the provisional best tabular classifier (XGBoost, AUC 0.8415) by a wide margin, though this gap should not be over-interpreted given the tabular number's contamination. The unsupervised BiLSTM-AE anomaly-screening endpoint (AUC 0.7545) is deliberately not positioned as competing with either supervised alternative on raw discrimination (see §7).

## 4. Sensor configuration implications

Across twelve tested sensor subsets, tabular classification AUC clusters into only three distinct values (approximately 0.780, 0.763, and 0.643), with several subsets — notably the full four-sensor configuration and reduced head+lower-back configurations — tying exactly at AUC 0.7796. This near-total insensitivity to sensor count beyond a two-sensor (head + lower back) minimum is consistent with §2's finding that lower-back signal complexity dominates feature importance. We flag this as a pattern worth re-verifying on the corrected N=260 cohort rather than reporting a specific sensor-efficiency conclusion at this stage. *(Provisional — `sensor_ablation.csv` via the contaminated tabular loader.)*

Separately, BiLSTM-AE sensor ablation (unaffected path) reports Voisard LOSO ensemble AUCs of 0.6856 (4-sensor), 0.7139 (HE+LB), and 0.6858 (LB-only), with zero-shot DAPHNET LB reconstruction 0.7046 for the 4-sensor-trained model.

## 5. Cross-cohort transfer and split-protocol sensitivity

Several cohort-held-out folds (ACL, CIPN, CVA, and others) produce undefined AUC due to single-class test folds. We do not report cross-cohort external-validity claims from the current leave-one-cohort-out (LOCO) results; combined with the PD-group contamination (held-out PD n=33 = 24 Voisard + 9 DAPHNET; Results §2b), this analysis requires re-running on corrected data before any generalization claim can be supported.

Independently, comparing strict participant-grouped LOSO against ungrouped StratifiedKFold shows consistent, modest AUC inflation under the leakage-prone protocol (mean ≈ 1.8%, range 0.0–5.3% across models; MLP max 5.3%). This supports the methodological argument that grouped evaluation matters for this data modality — a conclusion we retain qualitatively even under the tabular pipeline's provisional status, since it concerns protocol sensitivity rather than absolute AUC.

### In-domain vs. zero-shot fusion behavior

Under strict LOSO on Voisard, the source-locked 2-method ensemble (Isolation Forest + One-Class SVM on BiLSTM-AE latents, rank-averaged) slightly exceeds Isolation Forest alone (AUC 0.7545 vs. 0.7540; +0.0005) and substantially exceeds AE reconstruction error (0.4790). The ensemble is reported as the protocol primary endpoint but is empirically IF-dominated; OCSVM contributes negligible in-domain signal. Under zero-shot transfer to DAPHNET, applying the identical percentile-rank-averaging *procedure* independently on DAPHNET's pooled window scores (no DAPHNET-side tuning; ranks relative to DAPHNET's own 15,916-window pool, not a shared frozen Voisard reference), this ordering reverses: reconstruction error attains the higher point estimate (AUC 0.7046 vs. 0.5314 for the ensemble and 0.5884 for IF), though the 95% confidence intervals overlap. We deliberately did not retune fusion weights on DAPHNET, since doing so would use target-domain performance to select the model — precisely the leakage a zero-shot evaluation is designed to exclude. Fusion membership itself was source-locked from Voisard OOF diagnostics (not pre-registered before those diagnostics). The two ensembles therefore share membership and the rank-average formula, but not a strictly identical numeric scale. We report the reversal as-is.

One plausible explanation is that the latent-space one-class boundaries (IF, OCSVM) are fit to the shape of Voisard's healthy latent distribution — a function of its sensor layout, population, and acquisition protocol — and that boundary does not transfer cleanly to DAPHNET's zero-padded, single-sensor input and different patient population. Reconstruction error, by contrast, is a coarser, more distribution-agnostic signal ("does this resemble normal gait at all") and appears to degrade more gracefully under this domain shift. We present this as a plausible mechanism, not a demonstrated one; disentangling it from the identifiability limitation (all FOG+ windows from S03) would require FOG-positive data from more than one subject.

## 6. Reproducibility and reporting contribution

A practical contribution is the explicit reproducibility pathway: stage-structured execution, YAML configuration, deterministic seeding (`PYTHONHASHSEED=42` before Python start), lockfile-pinned CI installs, and containerized reruns. For clinical-ML literature, these engineering details affect trust and independent verification. Methodological disclosures in this manuscript — source-locked fusion membership, per-domain (not shared frozen) percentile ranking, laterality audit, and the tabular DAPHNET contamination with eval-only sensitivity — are intentional transparency choices.

## 7. Clinical interpretation boundary

These findings support pathology-tier gait screening as a research-oriented stratification aid, **not** direct prospective fall prediction. Labels are cohort-level diagnostic categories, not participant-level adjudicated future falls. The unsupervised endpoint is reported as primary because it requires no pathological labels at training time and remains deployable in populations without disease-specific training data — a property the supervised models (tabular or deep) do not share. Unsupervised deviation from the healthy manifold is not equivalent to epidemiological fall risk; orthopedic mechanical gait alterations (HOA, TKA, ACL) can fall within the healthy latent manifold — mean anomaly score gaps are negative for those cohorts — even when clinical fall-risk references are elevated.

## 8. Limitations and future directions

Key limitations are detailed in `docs/paper/limitations.md`: retrospective single-dataset design; no prospective fall-outcome endpoint; no external multi-site validation; laterality confound (Hip OA 15R/0L; CVA 47R/2L); provisional tabular results due to DAPHNET bleed into `patient_features.parquet`; and DAPHNET zero-shot FOG as a single-positive-subject (S03) case study. Future work: (i) Voisard-only tabular recompute (N=260), (ii) prospective validation with incident falls, (iii) external cohort replication with frozen models, (iv) cohort-shift calibration, (v) FOG evaluation with multiple positive-bearing subjects.

## 9. Conclusion

The pipeline demonstrates participant-grouped, reproducible wearable-IMU modeling with a primary Healthy-reference BiLSTM-AE anomaly endpoint (ROC-AUC 0.7545) and transparent zero-shot transfer disclosure. Supervised tabular numbers remain provisional until the corrected re-run; deep supervised baselines (clean N=260) and the primary anomaly path are the reliable numeric anchors for submission.
